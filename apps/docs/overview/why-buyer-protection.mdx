title: "Why buyer protection"
description: "x402 ends at the lock. Someone has to look after the buyer after that."
---

## Paying is solved, getting what you paid for is not

x402 lets software pay per request over plain HTTP. A server answers `402 Payment Required` with a price; the client pays and asks again. On Cardano, the `masumi` payment method makes that payment safer for the buyer: instead of going to the seller, the money is locked in an on-chain escrow under terms both sides signed.

The x402 Cardano scheme is explicit about where it stops. A settled escrow payment means the funds are **locked**, not delivered. Releasing them, refunding them or disputing them is left to whoever is watching.

## Nobody is watching

A person who pays for a file and gets nothing notices, complains and asks for a refund. An agent does not. It paid, it moved on, and the escrow deadlines pass without anyone acting. The seller can then collect money for something they never delivered.

Without protection, an agent can only buy from sellers its owner already trusts. That defeats the point of an open market where any agent can buy from any seller.

## What protection needs

To protect a buyer who is not watching, three things have to be true:

1. **The deal is fixed before payment.** The buyer must be able to prove, later, what it was promised. In Simpuru the seller commits to the hash of the content, and the escrow is bound to that hash. See [Content commitment](/learn/content-commitment).
2. **Someone acts on time.** Refunds and disputes are only possible inside deadlines. In Simpuru a protection watcher checks every purchase and acts on chain on its own. See [Protection watcher](/learn/protection-watcher).
3. **Disputes have a fair judge.** When buyer and seller disagree, the decision has to come from evidence, not from whoever shouts louder. In Simpuru the arbiter only looks at hashes neither side can change. See [Disputes and the arbiter](/learn/disputes-and-the-arbiter).
